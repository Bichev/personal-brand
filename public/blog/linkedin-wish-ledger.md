# LinkedIn Post: The Wish Ledger

---

In a Russian fairy tale printed in 1838, a girl rolls an apple around a silver saucer and it shows her cities, ships at sea and armies on the march.

I have one of those in my pocket. So do about six billion other people.

I wanted to know how far that goes, so I made a list. 125 wishes from the myths and fairy tales of more than 40 cultures: Slavic, Arabian, Greek, Egyptian, Indian, Chinese, Norse, Akan, Māori, K'iche' Maya and others. They sorted into ten kinds. To fly. To see far away. Servants that are made. A table that lays itself. Water that heals.

Then I checked each one against what exists today.

Eight of the ten have been granted, mostly to everybody. The oldest waited about 3,500 years. Jules Verne's Moon shot waited 103. Arthur C. Clarke's satellite waited 19.

I did not expect the warnings. The same people who wrote down the wish usually wrote down the bill. The sorcerer's apprentice is from about 150 CE. The Maya have a day when the cooking pots turn on their owners.

Every claim links to its source, and the last section covers the wishes still open.
